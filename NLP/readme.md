Here are the definitions for each of the NLP concepts based on the provided links:

1. **Tokenization**:
   Tokenization is the process of splitting a sentence or a body of text into smaller units, known as tokens. These tokens can be words, characters, or subwords. It helps to break down text into manageable chunks for further processing in natural language processing (NLP) tasks.
   - [Link](https://intellipaat.com/community/9025/tokenization-in-nlp)

2. **Stopwords**:
   Stopwords are common words such as "the", "is", "and", and "a" that are often filtered out in text processing. These words carry little value in terms of information retrieval or text analysis, but their treatment can vary depending on the specific NLP task at hand.
   - [Link](https://towardsdatascience.com/treat-negation-stopwords-differently-according-to-your-nlp-task-e5a59ab7c91f)

3. **Stemming**:
   Stemming is the process of reducing a word to its root form by removing prefixes or suffixes. For example, "running" would be stemmed to "run". It helps in normalizing words so that different forms of a word are treated as one.
   - [Link](https://searchenterpriseai.techtarget.com/definition/stemming)

4. **Speech Tagging (Part-of-Speech Tagging)**:
   Part-of-speech tagging (POS tagging) is the process of assigning a grammatical category (such as noun, verb, adjective) to each word in a sentence. This helps understand the role of words in sentences and is essential for other NLP tasks.
   - [Link](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/nlp-part-of-speech-default-tagging/)

5. **Chunking**:
   Chunking, in the context of NLP, refers to the process of segmenting a sentence into meaningful chunks such as noun phrases or verb phrases using patterns or regular expressions. It involves identifying and grouping words that form a meaningful unit.
   - [Link](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/nlp-chunking-and-chinking-with-regex/)

6. **Named Entity Recognition (NER)**:
   Named Entity Recognition is the process of identifying and classifying entities in text into predefined categories such as names of people, organizations, locations, dates, etc. It helps in understanding specific pieces of information from the text.
   - [Link](https://towardsdatascience.com/named-entity-recognition-3fad3f53c91e)

7. **Lemmatization**:
   Lemmatization is similar to stemming, but it involves reducing a word to its base or dictionary form (lemma), which is a valid word in the language. Unlike stemming, lemmatization considers the word's meaning and grammatical context to return the correct root form.
   - [Link](https://www.datacamp.com/community/tutorials/stemming-lemmatization-python)
